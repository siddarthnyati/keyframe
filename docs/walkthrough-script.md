# Keyframe walkthrough — 90-second voiceover

Three screens, one title launch. Target under 230 words. Read at a normal pace; pause where the line breaks.

---

Hi, I'm Sid. This is Keyframe, a small tool I built for one job: a producer has an approved trailer and needs the launch art set in every format and every language-locale, without finding out at upload that something fails spec.

Screen one, Shortlist. I drop the trailer. The browser samples frames, scores focus and exposure locally, folds duplicates, and sends the best 24 to a vision model for a designer's read: faces, mood, theme tags from the service's own taxonomy, and one sentence on why. I star three. The tool proposes, I decide.

Screen two, Variants. Each starred frame becomes a 2:3 poster, a 16:9 cover, a text-free hero and a 1:1 social, per locale. Every render carries the spec checks. Watch: I switch the title onto the hero, and the cell goes red. That is the whole point. The failure shows up here, not at upload. Export is a ZIP with a manifest.

Screen three, Audiences. Which variant each cohort sees, a bandit run forward two weeks, and the two numbers a marketer never gets back today: who clicked, and who kept watching.

My thesis: generation is not the gap. Selection, versioning, QC and feedback are.

What I would not build: generative art, paid-media optimisation, auto-approval, territory overrides.

I would rather hear where rejections surface today than show you more of this.
